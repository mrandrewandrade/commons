How Did the Phone Become This?

A NICE design story

Start here

A modern phone

Calls. Messages. Camera. Maps. Music. Games. Internet. Payment. School. Work.

How did one object end up doing all of this?

Rewind

Before the smartphone, before the mobile phone, before the telephone:

People still needed to communicate.

The situation was different. The need was the same.

Before electronics

People used visible or audible signals across distance:

fire and smoke

flags and semaphore

bells, horns, and drums

A signal could travel farther than a voice, but the message was limited.

A string and two cups

A cup-and-string phone is not the ancestor of the cellphone.

It is a simple model of the problem:

How can sound move from one person to another?

Sound vibrates the cup. The string carries the vibration. The second cup turns it back into sound.

Useful idea, serious limitation: you are still connected by a line.

Telegraph

Electricity made it possible to send a signal quickly through a wire.

New capability: long-distance messages could arrive much faster.

Limitation: the signal had to be encoded and decoded.

Design changed because the available technology changed.

Telephone

The telephone changed the question:

What if the wire could carry a voice?

People could speak naturally over long distances.

But the phone was tied to a place.

Radio

Radio changed another part of the situation:

What if communication did not need a wire?

Radio allowed communication while moving and where wires were impractical.

The trade-offs included equipment size, power, range, interference, and cost.

Field radio

Military, emergency, aviation, marine, and industrial communication pushed radio toward portable systems.

Some early portable systems were backpack-sized.

They proved something important:

Wireless voice communication could move with the user.

But portable did not yet mean pocket-sized.

Inquiry means looking sideways

A designer does not only ask, “What exists in phones?”

They also ask:

  • What does radio already do well?
  • What does the telephone network already do well?
  • What has the military learned about portable communication?
  • What can batteries and electronics now make possible?

Innovation often comes from combining existing ideas.

Telephone + radio

Telephone networks were good at connecting one person to another.

Radio was good at communication without a fixed wire.

What happens when those ideas come together?

Mobile telephone

The brick phone

The first useful mobile phones solved the biggest need:

Make a call while away from a landline.

But evaluation found obvious problems:

  • large
  • heavy
  • expensive
  • limited battery life

So the NICE cycle continued.

The pocket phone

The question changed from:

Can a telephone be mobile?

To:

Can I comfortably carry it everywhere?

Nokia-era phones became smaller, tougher, simpler, and better at lasting through the day.

Then people started typing

Voice was no longer enough.

Text messages and email changed what people wanted from a phone.

New need: enter information quickly.

BlackBerry-style devices used a full physical keyboard because typing had become important.

The keyboard solved a problem

A physical keyboard made typing easier.

But it also created a new limitation:

The keyboard always takes up space.

Even when you are looking at a photo, map, video, game, or webpage.

Evaluation creates the next design question.

Touchscreen

A touchscreen lets the same surface change jobs.

It can become:

keyboard | map | camera controls | game controls | browser | video

The phone is no longer only a telephone.

It becomes a small general-purpose computer.

Apps change the situation again

Instead of deciding every future function when the phone is built, software can add new functions later.

The user may discover needs they could not have asked for before the technology existed.

Good design can reveal a need, not only respond to one.

Consolidation

One device begins replacing many separate objects:

phone

camera

music player

GPS

calculator

calendar

flashlight

web browser

The design becomes a platform.

And then it spreads out again

Today, some phone functions move onto other devices:

smartwatch

earbuds

car displays

wearable sensors

The situation changes again: sometimes the best screen is the one you do not need to take out of your pocket.

The phone was never one invention

It was many cycles of:

Situation

N -> I -> C -> E

Changing needs. New research. New materials. New technologies. New prototypes. New feedback.

Then another cycle.

NICE

N: Needs & Necessities

What must it do? What would make it better?

I: Inquire & Investigate

What can we learn from users, existing designs, experiments, and other fields?

C: Create & Communicate

What is the smallest useful version we can make and show?

E: Evaluate

Did it work? Are the requirements still right? Did the situation change?

Design is a loop

Situation -> N -> I -> C -> E -> repeat

The goal is not to predict the perfect final product.

The goal is to make the next decision better than the last one.
