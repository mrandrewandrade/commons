A2 terms
Acceleration

Acceleration is how quickly velocity changes with time.

Acceleration describes how quickly velocity changes. A change in speed, direction, or both is acceleration. Its common SI unit is metres per second squared (m/s²). An acceleration of 1 m/s² means the velocity changes by 1 m/s every second.

Ampere

Also called: A, amp, amps

The ampere (A) is the SI unit of electric current.

The ampere, symbol A, is the SI unit used to measure electric current. In basic circuit work, current values are stated in amperes or amps.

Current

Also called: Electric Current, I

Current is the rate at which electric charge flows through a circuit.

Electric current is the rate at which electric charge flows through a circuit. The common quantity symbol is I and the SI unit is the ampere (A). More current means more electric charge is moving past a point each second.

E1 terms
Engineering Design Process

Also called: Design Process, EDP

The engineering design process is an iterative way to identify needs, create solutions, test them, and improve them.

The engineering design process is a flexible, iterative approach to solving problems. A typical cycle identifies the problem and needs, investigates existing information and solutions, develops ideas, creates or prototypes a solution, tests and evaluates it, communicates results, and improves the design. The process can loop backward or repeat whenever new evidence changes what the design needs.

Metres per second (m/s) is a unit of speed and velocity.

Metres per second, written m/s, tells how many metres of distance or displacement are covered each second. It is a common SI unit for both speed and velocity.

N.I.C.E. Design Process

Also called: N.I.C.E.

N.I.C.E. is a classroom design cycle: Needs & Necessities → Investigate & Inquire → Create & Communicate → Evaluate → repeat.

The N.I.C.E. Design Process is a classroom design cycle used in these resources: Needs & Necessities → Investigate & Inquire → Create & Communicate → Evaluate → repeat. It begins with what the design actually needs to accomplish, uses inquiry and investigation before choosing a solution, makes and communicates the solution, and uses evaluation to return to the needs and improve the next version.

Ohm

Also called: ohms

The ohm (Ω) is the SI unit of electrical resistance.

The ohm, symbol Ω, is the SI unit used to measure electrical resistance. In a simple resistive circuit, 1 ohm of resistance allows 1 ampere of current when 1 volt is applied.

P1 terms
Power

Also called: P

Power is the rate at which energy is transferred or used.

Power describes how quickly energy is transferred or used. The SI unit is the watt (W). In a DC electrical circuit, power can be calculated with P = V × I, where V is voltage and I is current.

R1 terms
Resistance

Also called: R

Resistance is opposition to electric current.

Electrical resistance describes how strongly a component or material opposes electric current. The common quantity symbol is R and the SI unit is the ohm (Ω). For a simple resistive circuit, voltage, current, and resistance are related by V = I × R.

Speed describes how quickly distance changes with time.

Speed describes how quickly an object covers distance. It has magnitude but no direction. A common SI unit is metres per second (m/s).

Velocity

Velocity describes how quickly displacement changes and includes direction.

Velocity is the rate of change of displacement and includes direction. A common SI unit is metres per second (m/s). Changing speed, changing direction, or both means the velocity is changing.

Volt

Also called: V, volts

The volt (V) is the SI unit of electric potential difference.

The volt, symbol V, is the SI unit used to measure electric potential difference, commonly called voltage. One volt is one joule of energy per coulomb of electric charge.

Voltage

Also called: Potential Difference

Voltage is electric potential difference: energy transferred per unit charge.

Voltage is the electric potential difference between two points. It describes energy transferred per unit charge. The SI unit is the volt (V). In introductory circuit work, voltage is sometimes described as electrical push, but potential difference is the more precise idea.
